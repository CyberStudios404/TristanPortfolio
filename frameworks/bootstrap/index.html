01 — ENGINEERING BRIEF

The problem

INITIALIZATION

Startup became a dependency problem.

As Ashes Engine expanded, more systems depended on services, registries and persistent state being available before they could safely operate. Scene-level initialization created implicit ordering assumptions and made the runtime lifecycle harder to reason about.

Bootstrap was introduced to make that lifecycle explicit and keep engine infrastructure separate from the scenes that consume it.

06 — WHY THIS / WHY NOT

The architecture
was a choice.

WHY BOOTSTRAP?

Explicit lifecycle ownership

Startup becomes an engine responsibility instead of being scattered across whatever scene happens to load.

WHY NOT SCENE INIT?

Scenes are content boundaries

Gameplay scenes should describe content and runtime composition rather than reconstructing engine infrastructure.

WHY NOT ONE GAME MANAGER?

Avoid broad ownership

A single manager can become a dependency hub with unclear responsibility and increasingly difficult testing boundaries.

WHY DEFER WORK?

Not every system belongs on the critical path

Work that does not affect initial usability can be scheduled separately from the minimum startup sequence.

07 — STARTUP SEQUENCE

Initialization is a lifecycle.

The important distinction is between work required to make the application usable and work that can continue afterward.

STEP 01

Bootstrap scene loads

The dedicated startup scene becomes the entry point for engine initialization.

STEP 02

Configuration is loaded

Foundational runtime data is prepared before systems that depend on it attempt initialization.

STEP 03

Registries and services initialize

Core infrastructure becomes available to dependent systems.

STEP 04

Required dependencies are validated

The runtime verifies the infrastructure required by systems before treating them as ready.

STEP 05

Menu becomes available

Player-facing presentation does not unnecessarily wait for every non-critical subsystem.

STEP 06

Deferred systems continue

Background initialization proceeds where the architecture allows it to remain off the critical startup path.

08 — ENGINEERING DECISIONS

Architecture is mostly
trade-offs.

REASON

Hardcoded values

Trade-off

Explicit lifecycle management adds structure and therefore adds some complexity. The trade is intentional: as the engine accumulates persistent systems, predictable ownership becomes more valuable than minimizing initialization code.

17 — ENGINEERING SUMMARY

Why Bootstrap exists.

Bootstrap makes Ashes Engine's runtime lifecycle explicit instead of allowing initialization to emerge from scene ordering and hidden dependencies.

Its responsibility is intentionally narrow: establish the infrastructure required by the application, validate the environment those systems expect, and allow player-facing content to become available without unnecessarily blocking on non-critical work.

The architecture is still evolving. The remaining work is primarily around stronger diagnostics, profiling and formal validation rather than turning Bootstrap into a monolithic controller.
